# Campaign research: what each campaign asks of a deck

The notes in `lib/campaigns.ts` say what each campaign asks of a deck. A claim made it into a
note only if it was verified: two independent sources agree, it is a rule in the Rules Reference
(`data/rules.md`), or a count of the campaign's encounter cards shows it. Story structure that
changes nothing in a deck is left out, as are plot, bosses and endings.

The sources were the Rules Reference, published deckbuilding guides and reviews, Fantasy Flight's
previews, and players' advice on the archived FFG forums and on Reddit. Researched October 2026.

## Left out

Claims that only one source made, that sources disagreed on, or that the counts contradict:

- **Night of the Zealot:** covering all four skills between the investigators (one source).
- **The Dunwich Legacy:** "more horror than damage" (the counts show the opposite).
- **The Path to Carcosa:** "less willpower-heavy than Dunwich" (the counts show the opposite).
- **The Dream-Eaters:** swarming weakens single-target weapons; the two halves favor different
  roles (one source each).
- **The Innsmouth Conspiracy:** the cycle favors evasion (players warn that evading Deep Ones
  repeatedly costs you).
- **Edge of the Earth:** how often a second frost token turns up (players disagree); "few but
  impactful enemies" (the counts show many).
- **The Scarlet Keys:** don't spend ammo or charges exposing; quick-setup decks beat asset
  engines (one source each).
- **The Feast of Hemlock Vale:** succeed by 4 or 5 (one source).
- **The Drowned City:** avoid low sanity (the counts point the other way); heavy on discard and
  plenty of XP (one source each).
